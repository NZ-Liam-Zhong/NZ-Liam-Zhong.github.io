Overview

Foundation models such as GPT-6 Astra can now output robot commands directly. Fine-tuned policies are evaluated on a handful of precise tasks; models like these need many real tasks. EgoSim builds them from videos: every video becomes a simulated scene, tasks grounded in what happens in it, and a validated evaluation. A vision-language model (VLM) decides what things are; geometry, scale and poses come from depth, masks and multi-view voting, so the pipeline runs without per-video tuning.

Method

1 · Observe and register

  • Plan. The VLM describes the support (type, SAM prompt, outline shape, structure, height prior, sides, periodic print) and the floor from sharp frames spread over the video.
  • Reference frame and world. The sharpest frame with few hands; a RANSAC plane on its DA3 points inside the SAM 3 support mask defines z = 0. Every frame is registered to that plane by a homography, so masks from all frames can be voted in one metric map.
  • Height and scale. Floor points give the support height; VLM sizes against measured footprints rescale the world when DA3's metric scale is off.

2 · Objects and support

  • Inventory. Names, SAM prompts, kind (flat / lathe / generic), size, colour, mass, cloth parameters.
  • Locate. SAM 3 masks on all registered frames, warped to the plane and voted: footprint, diameter, height from how far each silhouette reaches away from its camera, rectified top texture.
  • Build. Flat → textured slab; lathe → revolved profile; generic → product photo → Hunyuan3D-2.1 mesh scaled to the measurement; garments → particle cloth.
  • Verify. Meshes projected into frames vs SAM 3 masks; the VLM returns bounded size corrections.
  • Support. Texture fused from every frame where no hand or object covers it, unseen parts continued along the print's lattice; the floor tile comes from the video too.
  • Render–critic. The scene is rendered from the video's camera and compared with the real frame: a per-cell colour check of the support (which overrules VLM complaints the video contradicts), a colour gain, and a VLM critic whose findings become fixes (refill, retexture, floor, locate, build) for up to 2 rounds.

3 · Author the room

Following AHa-3D, but for OmniGibson / Isaac Sim instead of Blender, the scene around the support is authored rather than reconstructed point by point:

  • Room. The VLM names the structures around the support; their fused depth gives vertical planes (pooled RANSAC, coplanar merge, Manhattan snap, joined corners), each a wall with a collider.
  • Materials. Video-first: the fused support top and floor tile when the video saw enough of them, otherwise procedural presets in the measured colours; no baked lighting.
  • Pose fit. Every static object's x, y, yaw and scale fitted to SAM 3 silhouettes in several frames.
  • Calibrate. One render-to-video colour gain for all materials.

Cloth

From scene to task

Mine

The VLM watches frames spread over the video: is it a human's egocentric recording, and which manipulation events happen (verb, objects, target, frame span)? Each event is judged simulable (pick, place, push, stack, put into an open container) or not (opening a can, pouring, buttoning).

Propose

Egocentric videos with simulable events give video tasks; robot or third-person footage, or videos with nothing simulable, fall back to tasks the VLM proposes for the scene. Facts the VLM cannot see are supplied: sizes, positions in the robot's table frame, reach, graspability.

Goals

A fixed predicate vocabulary, checked on simulator state:

on_top(a, b) · inside(a, b) · in_region(a, near|far|left|right|corners|center) · near(a, b, d_cm) · upright(a)

near counts the free space between the two objects (edge to edge), as a person reads "within 9 cm of the can".

Physical checks

1Goal is false now

The initial state must not already satisfy the goal.

2Goal is reachable and stable

A free, reachable goal pose that still holds when shifted 2 cm (a near band of a few cm excepted); objects teleported there and settled by physics must satisfy the goal.

3An expert run

A privileged scripted top-down pick-and-place runs on the task and is reported next to the model. Checks 1–2 decide validity; the expert is not yet a hard gate because its IK cannot reach close to the robot's base.

On ThrowAwayTrash: "within 14 cm of the bin" was rejected at check 2 (only a 12–14 cm ring around a 17 cm bin is left); "the cup on the bin's closed lid" passes all three, with the expert lifting the cup onto the 27 cm lid; "bring the cup forward" passes checks 1–2 while the expert stops 4.4 cm short. A visual judge cannot see any of this.

Closed-loop evaluation

A Franka Panda stands on a pedestal on the operator's side of the table (where the video was filmed from), with an IK controller on absolute end-effector poses and the fingers pointing down. Each call, the model receives three images, the gripper's pose and its recent actions; in state mode also the objects' positions. It answers with up to four actions:

{"reasoning": "...",
 "actions": [{"type": "move", "xyz_cm": [x, y, z], "yaw_deg": 90},
             {"type": "close"}, {"type": "open"}],
 "done": false}

Coordinates are in the table frame (cm): origin at the table centre, x to the robot's right, y away from it, z up. Budget: 20 calls. Stages: 0 nothing · 1 grasped · 2 lifted · 3 over the goal · 4 goal holds after release.

Results

Two tasks of the benchmark (ThrowAwayTrash, proposed, valid), one trial each. Task: "Place the blue cup upright in the far-left area, using a spot within your reach rather than the back corner."

Task: "Carefully stand the blue cup upright on top of the bin's closed lid", a lift onto a 27 cm lid.

What the physical checks caught

Inconsistent definitions

near was checked centre to centre while placement kept footprints apart, so "the glass within 9 cm of the can" had no feasible pose and every such task was rejected. Both now use the same footprint gap; the tasks were proposed again.

Simulator settings

OmniGibson's cloth grasp flag silently disabled rigid grasps; a 25° gripper tilt came with the robot's reset pose.

False successes

A reset that did not restore teleported objects made an early expert run look successful and a stable lid goal look unstable. Rollouts now refuse to start if the goal already holds.

Single trials on one easy task show that the loop works end to end; the success rates are in the benchmark below.

Reproduce

One NVIDIA RTX PRO 6000 Blackwell (96 GB), SimFoundry 9e34ebe + the patch in patches/, Python 3.11, PyTorch 2.7, OmniGibson 3.8.0.

git clone https://github.com/NZ-Liam-Zhong/EgoSim.git && cd EgoSim
bash scripts/install.sh ~/SimFoundry        # SimFoundry 9e34ebe + EgoSim patch
# environments + checkpoints: ~/SimFoundry/docs/AGENT_INSTALL.md
cp scripts/api_keys.example.txt ~/SimFoundry/api_keys.txt     # OPENAI_API_KEY, HF_TOKEN
cp egosim/env.local.example.sh egosim/env.local.sh            # python env paths
bash scripts/doctor.sh                                        # all [ok]

bash egosim/egosim.sh /abs/path/video.mp4    # scene, tasks, evaluation; the ablation and judge come with it

Every step leaves a .done_<step> marker, so commands resume. The repository keeps every stage as its own command; see the README, and AGENTS.md for coding agents.

Toward a benchmark of real tasks

Scale

~1000 videos → ~1000 validated tasks, with per-stage failure statistics as a result in itself.

Embodiments

Franka today; YAM, R1 and Tiago are in OmniGibson; export to Isaac Lab-Arena and RoboVerse.

Physics in the loop

Check failures fed back into reconstruction (colliders, overlaps, scale) and task proposal.
